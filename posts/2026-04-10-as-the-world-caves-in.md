*This was written as a companion piece to my good friend's diary of Marathon des Sables' stage 5 when we ran it together. Read [here](https://www.moveandmeet.de/posts/mds-legendary-stage-5) for a much better example of storytelling!*

Yesterday after finishing the monumental 100k long stage, the entire camp was greeted with the worst sandstorm of the trip so far. Even inside the tent with one side completely sheltered to the ground, sand was coming through to my face. Cooking wasn't possible with the wind, going outside wasn't possible without getting showered by the sand, so for once I didn't visit my friends from the other tents and just stayed in mine. I thought I'd finally get good sleep after 30 hours of exercise, but the night ended up being the worst with the wind and the thoughts. 0 sleep, again.

When I woke up, I was glad it was over. But also, I was mentally ready to quit. The first stage, I was driven by the excitement of the race and to figure out my hydration. The second stage, everything fell apart: my shoe and my toe broke; all I wanted was to drop out and see how Annee was doing. The third stage: I was a day away from the 100k, so I really wanted to be there to test myself. But now this fifth stage, what was the purpose? I had already finished the 100k, I just want to sleep again, to have a sink, to be able to enjoy the simple luxuries of normal life. These were all in my head: I was gone.

When the alarm hit at 4:30am, I had my usual overnight oats in the red light silently with a couple of my other tentmates that were already awake. The two beside me, Mike and Richard usually sleep an extra 30 minutes before waking up. When they did, had my usual morning catch-up with Richard to discuss how we slept and how we felt about the day. He sensed that I was different today for sure. After brekkie, I slowly dressed up to my race gear, still unsure of what to do. Organized the backpack like usual, and right before we were all called to the starting line at 6am, I went to the toilet. The music blared from the line, the crowd more silent than before, but it was calling. When I arrived back to my tent, I expected that everyone would be gone to the starting line, but Richard was there waiting for me today.

***"We'll make it together. I'll see you tonight."***

That was all I needed. Today was just a 42k marathon, simple. Just this stage, one more sleep, and then it's the last stage and a hotel. And it flew.

When I came back to tent, everyone was in a more celebratory mood. There was an understanding that we were almost at the end of this longest race, this camping life in the desert, these conditions in the highway to hell (a song that was played before we started each stage). Everyone was chatting about how we did, until suddenly there was an announcement by a volunteer (blue jacket) that they were offering fresh fruits by the finish line in a few minutes. We all hurried over, as we were all deprived of fresh food for a week and a half at this point. A queue of hundreds of people quickly formed by multiple vans, and was efficiently taken care of, ending with a lot of happy people with an orange and a banana. I had the orange with my tentmates, and I can tell you it was the best one I've ever had in my life. It made the day of suffering worth it. After awhile, even though I was satiated by the fruits and my brioche rolls I still had to force myself to eat more and get my dinner ready. After cooking up the hot water and putting it in my pasta instant meal, I was able to find time to visit tent 189. On the first day, I went there since that was where Annee was and it turned out it was also where all my friends that I met from before the race was as well. Kept it quick today, since I wanted to spend more time with my 6 tentmates since it was our last one together. Dinner was terrible but bearable and edible, did the bedtime routine of; toilet, cleaning myself with wet wipes, stretching, and then we were all in our sleeping bags chatting away. For one last time, we all just kept our eyes open in silence under the stars.

![camp](camp.jpg "Camping life in the desert")

It was at this moment that I realised today was when I truly appreciated being here. I was reminded of what Natasha said to me when I was complaining to her about the race during the 100k, and she had this to say to me:

***"Turn off your headtorch and look up at the stars. Who else gets to do this?"***

She was right. At that moment, I wanted this to last forever. This morning, I would have given everything to be driven 6 hours away back to civilization for a working sink. I think a week from now, a year from now, a life from now: I would give everything to be back at this exact moment. We may not have showered for a week and a half, we may all smell terrible, our feet may no longer work after this, our friendships may be temporary, there were a lot of pain, disappointments, heartbreaks, and finish line tears. But that's all I want.
